# Client Intake Questionnaire for Freelance Developers

Use this before accepting a new project. The goal is to reveal scope, risk,
decision speed, and whether the client can actually support the work.

## 1. Business Context

1. What business problem should this project solve?
2. What happens if this project is not completed in the next 30-90 days?
3. Who owns the final decision?
4. Who will give feedback during the project?
5. What does a successful outcome look like in plain business terms?

## 2. Users and Workflow

1. Who will use the final product or feature?
2. What are they using today instead?
3. What are the most common user mistakes or complaints?
4. Which workflow should become faster, cheaper, or less error-prone?
5. Are there accessibility, language, or device requirements?

## 3. Scope

1. What must be included in version one?
2. What is explicitly out of scope?
3. Which third-party services, APIs, or accounts are involved?
4. Do you already have designs, copy, brand assets, or data?
5. Which environments are required: local, staging, production?

## 4. Technical Reality

1. Where is the current code hosted?
2. Who can grant repository, hosting, analytics, and domain access?
3. What stack, framework, or CMS is already in use?
4. Are there known bugs, performance issues, or security concerns?
5. Is there test coverage or deployment automation?

## 5. Timeline and Budget

1. What deadline matters and why?
2. Is the deadline flexible if scope changes?
3. What budget range has already been approved?
4. Who approves extra work?
5. What would make you pause or cancel the project?

## 6. Risk Signals

Mark any that apply:

- No single decision owner.
- Deadline is fixed but scope is not.
- Budget is unknown.
- Access to systems is not available.
- Requirements depend on another vendor.
- Existing code has no deployment path.
- The client wants a guaranteed business result.

## 7. Next Step

After reviewing the answers, send one of three responses:

- Accept and propose a fixed first milestone.
- Offer a paid discovery session.
- Decline politely because scope, access, budget, or risk is not workable.
